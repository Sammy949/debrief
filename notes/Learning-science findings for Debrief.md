# Learning-science findings for Debrief

## Retrieval practice

The UC San Diego Psychology resource describes retrieval practice as recalling information from memory rather than rereading it. It emphasizes checking the recalled explanation against source material and repeating the process. It also notes that application questions are appropriate when the real performance context requires application.

Design implication: Debrief should begin with explanation from memory, then provide a targeted check and a second attempt. The repair round should use application or “why” prompts, not merely ask the learner to repeat a definition.

Source: https://psychology.ucsd.edu/undergraduate-program/undergraduate-resources/academic-writing-resources/effective-studying/retrieval-practice.html

## Generative learning

Fiorella’s 2023 review in Educational Psychology Review describes generative learning as actively organizing and integrating new material with existing knowledge. It distinguishes explaining, visualizing, and enacting as complementary sense-making modes, and argues that learners may need explicit prompts and guidance to engage in those activities effectively.

Design implication: Debrief should not stop at “write an explanation.” It should guide the learner through a sequence of explanation, mental or visual structure, and application. The understanding map can support visualization, while the follow-up and repair round support explanation and enactment.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09769-7

## Product conclusion

Debrief is best understood as a guided learning loop, not an evaluation-only tool. It teaches by making the learner generate an explanation, revealing a specific gap, giving a small amount of just-in-time instruction, and asking the learner to apply or re-explain the repaired idea. The system should distinguish between diagnosis and teaching so the learner does not receive only a judgment.
